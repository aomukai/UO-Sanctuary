# Cindermaw's hoard

Built-in ImageGen produced [the weathered hoard](cindermaw-hoard-weathered.png) by editing the concept supplied in `hoard.png` with a screenshot of the lair for scale. Run `python3 tools/build_dragon_hoard.py` to pack it into ClassicUO loose art.

The [ground scatter](cindermaw-hoard-ground.png) and [lower slope detail](cindermaw-hoard-slope.png) are separate from the nest. The [clearing mask](clearing-mask.png) comes from a terrain-only ClassicUO capture aligned to the nest at (1445, 902). It clips the scatter at the grass boundary. The packer writes the art and a [relative placement template](../../../data/Sanctuary/dragon-hoard-template.json) that keeps all the pieces aligned.

Game masters can use `[DragonHoard` and target the tile where the nest should sit, or use `[DragonHoard x y` with an optional `z`. Choose a broad clearing of similar shape and elevation. The nest and ground scatter are one walkable decoration. Place the loot chest separately where the site calls for one.

## Ground scatter prompts

> Create one wide ground-decoration sprite for an original late-1990s isometric fantasy RPG, genuinely transparent RGBA. The first reference is the existing dragon clutch sprite and establishes the matte, hand-painted pixel style and restrained brass/straw/bone palette. The second reference is an actual gameplay screenshot, and is for grass-clearing shape, camera angle, and scale only. The existing clutch will stay separate and be drawn on top in the right half. Create the surrounding floor clutter alone, enough to span the whole approximately 700 by 550 native pixel clearing without magnifying any one object. Make it a broad irregular slanted oval/diamond of old straw drifts and scattered tiny tarnished bronze coins, with numerous small overlapping patches, a few palm-sized skulls and thin bone fragments, two or three bits of rusted ordinary armor or cut leather, and a little spent kindling. Keep skulls no larger than about 1.3 percent of the entire sprite width (8 to 9 native pixels), coins around 0.3 percent, and any individual armor piece smaller than a human torso. The terrain floor should read as covered with material across the footprint, with many see-through irregular gaps that let underlying green grass show through, fine stray straw at the edge, some denser low patches and other thinner areas. Asymmetrically distribute the patches across the full width and height like detritus pushed outward by a nesting animal. Slightly flatten layout to match the game's fixed 2:1 isometric camera. No separate centerpiece, no nest, eggs, chests, flags, candlesticks, scenery, creatures, moss, grass itself, visible dirt background, black backdrop, shadow halo, title, or labels. Render only the shallow debris over a truly transparent background. The faint loose edges and the generous transparent holes must survive a downsample to ~700 pixels.

> Refine the first reference, the sparse weathered ground scatter from the previous pass, to fit the full green clearing in the second reference. On the second image, green grass is the exact extent of the decoration, bounded by rock and the entry path. Output just the decorative elements as true transparent RGBA. Make the perimeter a taller irregular isometric footprint, with a wider band of debris toward upper right and a trail that tapers into the low left and bottom point of the grassy clearing. Distribute close, low whorls of straw and tiny tarnished coins from the northern edge all the way down to the southern tip, including the big western open area. Let underlying grass through in many small gaps. Substitute small scattered slivers of bone and rusted equipment fragments for the large skulls and intact armor. Ordinary coin specks and straw are the dominant scale. Include no newly composed central object, eggs, chests, banners, complete humanoid skulls, living creatures, ground color, grass texture, scenery, or baked-in glow. Keep the crisp subdued pixel-painted style, excellent source detail and matte brown-bronze palette. Every pixel beyond the individual surface debris should have zero alpha.

## Weathered hoard prompts

> Edit the first image, hoard.png, into a production-ready original game sprite for the grassy clearing in the second image. The second image is a scale, ground angle, and palette reference only. Keep the broad irregular ground footprint, isometric camera, human scale, dragon eggs nestled in straw, small scattered coins, discarded armor and weapons, and dry skull piles and bones. Make it feel like an old, inhabited animal den in a serious late-1990s isometric fantasy RPG. Reduce ornament and saturation substantially. Trade glowing gold for aged brass and small tarnished coins piled thinly in irregular drifts with transparent patches where grass will show through. Use one or two plain ironbound wooden chests, scuffed steel and leather, bone and muted olive straw. Make the egg clutch the focus, a few muted slate, umber, and ivory eggs with naturally varied shells. Remove the heraldic banners, ceremonial drapery, elaborate candle displays, jewel confetti, and fleshy bodies completely. Retain dry bones for the signs of past kills. Use restrained daylight, crisp hand-painted pixel clusters and matte weathered materials, with the level of contrast and camera angle of the reference game. Keep the objects and their natural variegated edges fully in frame. Output exactly one isolated, transparent RGBA hoard sprite. All ground, grass, rock, lighting haze and glow, shadows beyond the objects, scenery, living creatures, and UI are outside the asset and must have zero alpha. Aim for the hoard to remain readable at about 330 native game pixels wide; skulls must read at the scale of the Avatar's head.

> Edit the first reference, the weathered dragon den artwork, in two precise ways. Erase both wooden chests and their metal fittings completely. In the vacated spots, continue the broken-up scatter of old coins and thin straw, with irregular clear gaps punched through to real transparency. Make each human skull and jawbone on both sides and the foreground about 45 percent of its current width and height. A skull should be about 2 to 2.5 percent of the entire sprite width, a mere 7 to 8 pixels when the sprite is displayed 330 pixels wide, matching an Avatar's skull in the second screenshot. Keep those much smaller skulls grouped low on the ground and allow some straw or coins between them. Do not replace them with oversized animal skulls. Preserve the three slate, umber, and ivory eggs and their straw clutch as the main motif, the discarded martial gear, muted daylight palette, low isometric perspective, and matte, pixel-painted fantasy RPG look. Keep the outer silhouette wide and irregular with clear transparent holes and no painted terrain. The scene consists solely of eggs, straw, coins, old discarded armor, weapons, and dry skeletal remnants. Use a genuine transparent RGBA background with zero alpha in the clear areas and around the exterior. The game screenshot is a scale and lighting reference, not part of the final image.
